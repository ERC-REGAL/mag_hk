What Ordering.lean checks

The radial differential expression and Riccati potential are concrete real
formulas. Lean proves both factorization formulas by the product rule
expressed on a function's two-jet. The radial-measure term in the formal
adjoint is retained. Both angular-sector difference identities and their
strict positivity for every integer sector other than zero and minus one
are proved.

DomainOperator keeps an operator's domain as a linear subspace. The
eigenvector transfer theorem proves that A(Dψ) belongs to Dom(D) when
A(Dψ) = Eψ. Thus application of D a second time is justified at the
abstract operator level. This statement applies with A equal to D*.

The energy arguments derive the zero-sector dichotomy from actual norm
and quadratic-form equalities. They do not assume that an excited energy
is above λ. The other sectors use a positive form correction and the
minus-one ground-state lower bound. The theorem for the quantitative
partner bound applies to the oscillator example with μ equal to four.

The spectral assembly proves that the only spectral values at or below λ
are zero and λ, and that they occur in exactly their designated sectors.
The multiplicity lemma combines radial ground-state simplicity with the
direct-sum eigenspace decomposition. The strict spectral gap follows from
isolation below the essential-spectrum threshold. This isolation input is
necessary. Pointwise strict form inequalities alone do not provide a
uniform gap across infinitely many angular sectors.

The logarithmic cutoff derivative and exact annulus Dirichlet integral
are formalized. The integral of 1/r on shrinking annuli is proved
arbitrarily large, which is the scalar integral behind both forbidden
origin branches. Equality of self-adjoint realizations is deduced from
uniqueness of an extension of the common core.

Analytic facts still supplied through explicit hypotheses

RadialRealization.lean instantiates RadialOrderingData in the actual
complex L²(r dr) space using literal quadratic-form integrals, a
distributional weak-derivative predicate with smooth compact tests, and
explicit operator domains. The general realization library is a
universal premise rather than a mathlib construction of unbounded
operators. Its facts give integral and domain identities, from which
the eigenvalue comparisons are proved. BoundedRealization.lean
discharges the concrete coefficient and solution hypotheses. The required
standard results are the Friedrichs form construction, passage from a
form core to the closed form, positivity and simplicity from the positive
radial ground-state representation, integration against a strictly
positive multiplier, and the adjoint/product domain facts for a closed
densely defined operator. These are standard operator or Sturm theory.

DiscreteSpectralAssembly uses the standard self-adjoint direct-sum
spectral theorem and discreteness below the essential-spectrum threshold.
It does not establish the relevant essential spectrum of either example.
Those concrete hypotheses belong to the example modules and their
analytic contracts.

The exact cutoff calculation does not by itself construct a sequence in
C_c∞, nor prove convergence of that sequence in the weighted graph norm.
The remaining passage uses local boundedness of U, smoothing on annuli,
the cutoff estimates, and density of smooth functions in the Sobolev
norm. The endpoint asymptotics are proved for both examples in
RadialEndpoints.lean and PartnerAsymptotics.lean. The limit-point
uniqueness theorem remains a general operator-theory input. PlanarEndpointAsymptotics records the general smooth Frobenius theorem
explicitly, with actual local solution branches, limits, Wronskians and
energy classifications. It instantiates both example coefficients.
The draft's hypotheses U = O(r) and smooth U yield the usual regular and
logarithmic branches for D*D and the r and 1/r branches for DD*. Bounded or quadratically growing potentials at
infinity give the required limit-point endpoint in these examples.

Mathematical finding

No contradictory ordering inequality was found. In the sector comparison
display, the expression <ψ,L₋₁ψ> should be interpreted as the closed
quadratic form q₋₁[ψ]. The preceding argument establishes only form-domain
membership of ψ, which need not be operator-domain membership. Replacing
that notation by q₋₁[ψ] makes the argument precise without changing it.

Scope

This file is a Lean verification of the algebra and conditional analytic
deductions described above. It is not a claim that mathlib constructs the
specific Friedrichs realizations, their Sobolev domains, or the endpoint
classification without analytic assumptions.

Standalone general lemma

OrderingTheorem.lowest_two_planar_levels now handles the standalone lemma
without the stronger endpoint hypotheses used for the two constructions.
It takes actual radial Friedrichs graphs, the closure of d/dr+U, its adjoint,
the D*D and DD* graph identities, the literal positive multiplication sum,
positive L2 eigenvectors and an actual unitary direct sum. The radial
zeroth-order potentials and positive correction are locally integrable on
the open half-line. This explicit technical interpretation of the differential
forms addresses coefficient regularity left implicit in the standalone
manuscript statement. No smoothness is imposed on the given eigenfunctions.
The universal ground-transform theorem supplies positive C1 representatives.
There are no endpoint bounds or separate orbital-energy premises. General
ground-state, closed-form and Sturm laws supply integral
identities, from which the proof derives RadialOrderingData and both simple
levels with a strict gap. The partner Friedrichs realization follows from
a universal positive selfadjoint sum theorem. Form energies are actual
form-norm limits, which avoids requiring separate kinetic and potential
integrability for general semibounded radial problems. The concrete endpoint
proofs and this abstract operator-domain route are separate applications.
